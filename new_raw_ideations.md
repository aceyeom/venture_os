## 001 — Verifiable Royalty Intelligence for Music-Backed Credit




- **Date / Run:** 2026-05-30 (Run 001)
- **One-liner:** Music-catalog funds, ABS arrangers, rating agencies & fund auditors × independent,
 song-level royalty *verification and surveillance* for music-backed debt/equity × the 2024–25
 explosion of music-backed securitization colliding with the Hipgnosis data-quality scandal.




- **The non-obvious insight:** The crowded space (Curve, Penny Royalty, Tone, Synchtank/IRIS,
 Reprtoir) is all **operator-side** — royalty accounting and artist payouts. Nobody serves the
 **capital side**: the lenders, ABS issuers, rating agencies, LPs, and auditors who must
 *independently verify* that a catalog's cashflows are real, song-level, complete, and
 collectible before lending/rating/buying. An issuer's own software is not trusted by its
 creditors; a neutral third-party verifier is exactly what the market lacks. This is an
 "MRV / surveillance layer" for a brand-new asset class, not another accounting tool.




- **Buyer & budget:** Catalog funds backed by Blackstone, Carlyle, Apollo, state pensions; the
 banks arranging $1B+ ABS deals (fee-rich); rating agencies running music-ABS surveillance;
 Big-4 + specialist auditors. All structurally flush and already pay six-figure+ subscriptions
 for analogous structured-credit data infra (Intex, Trepp, Markit/IHS). High ACV, recurring.




- **Why now (catalyst):** Music-backed *debt* hit a record **~$4.4B raised by Sept 2025**
 (Blackstone, Carlyle, Michigan pension). Landmark rated deals: Concord's **$1.8B A+ ABS**,
 Hipgnosis' **$1.47B securitization backed by 45,000 songs**. Simultaneously, the **Hipgnosis
 due-diligence blowup** revealed a $2.36B fund "not tracking the catalog at the song level,"
 with "multiple inconsistent versions of the same financial information" — a public, dated
 proof that unverified royalty data destroys NAV and trust. Rating agencies establishing the
 asset class now require *ongoing surveillance data* they can't source themselves.
 (Catalyst is **market-driven**, satisfying Rule R2 — not dependent on one administration.)




- **Wedge vs incumbents:** Operator-side royalty engines do calc + payout for publishers/labels.
 This platform serves a different buyer (capital), a different job (independent attestation,
 ABS waterfall surveillance, LP-grade NAV verification, underpayment/collection-gap recovery),
 and produces different outputs (rating-agency surveillance reports, audit-grade exception
 logs). It can ingest *from* the operator tools rather than compete with them. Horizontal
 structured-credit vendors (Intex, Bloomberg) don't touch the fragmented PRO/DSP/mechanical/
 sub-publisher royalty plumbing. (Resolves Rule R3 — explicitly the capital side.)




- **Moat:** Becoming the rating-agency-accepted verification *standard* (regulatory-like lock-in)
 + data network effects (more catalogs ingested → better valuation comps and statistical
 underpayment detection) + brutally deep normalization across thousands of PRO/DSP/sub-publisher
 statement formats and the 6–12 month royalty settlement lag.




- **TAM shape & expansion path:** Buyer count is small today (few dozen funds, a handful of ABS
 arrangers, 2–3 active raters) — the chief risk. Offset by very high ACV and clear expansion:
 (a) the much larger pool of catalogs needing audit/recovery, (b) adjacent rights-backed credit
 (film/TV residuals, franchise royalties, pharma & litigation royalties, whole-business
 securitization). Credible path to $50–150M ARR as the verification layer for rights-backed
 finance broadly.




- **How it survives the Gauntlet:**
 1. Painkiller — yes; mispriced/unverified cashflows = millions lost, deals unratable. ✅
 2. Funded buyer — capital-markets buyers, not poor operators; software *is* the value. ✅ (passes R1)
 3. Specific wedge — distinct buyer/job/output vs operator-side incumbents. ✅
 4. Distribution — every prospect is nameable; Hipgnosis is the warm-intro narrative. ✅
 5. Why now — record 2025 music-debt issuance + Hipgnosis scandal; market-driven. ✅ (passes R2)
 6. Defensibility — standard-setting + data network effects + integration depth. ✅
 7. TAM — small buyer set but high ACV + concrete expansion into rights-backed credit. ✅ (with risk noted)
 8. Originality — capital-side verification insight, validated by a real public failure. ✅




- **Top risks (honest):** (1) Small initial buyer universe = concentration risk; mitigate via
 high ACV + adjacency expansion. (2) Incumbents (e.g., Synchtank/IRIS) extending upward into
 fund-level reporting. (3) Rating agencies building in-house. (4) Data access depends on
 cooperation from administrators/PROs — must secure feeds early. Independent-third-party
 positioning is the key defense against (2) and (3): a counterparty's own tool isn't trusted
 by the other side of the trade.
